3.How a gig works

  1. A client posts a gig — the work needed, the location, the budget, and what "done" looks like.
  2. A professional claims it, or a client hires a specific professional directly from the directory.
  3. The professional does the work and submits it for the client's review.
  4. The client signs off, which releases payment, or raises a dispute if the work doesn't meet what was agreed.

Sorted connects clients and professionals and provides escrow, coordination and dispute support. Sorted is not a party to the underlying service agreement between a client and a professional, and doesn't perform the work itself.

4.Payments and escrow

When a client approves a professional's work, Sorted charges the client through a licensed payment partner (currently Paystack and/or Bachs) and splits the payment at the payment partner's end — the professional's share goes directly to them, and Sorted's fee is retained — so client funds never sit in Sorted's own account waiting to be paid out.

Sorted doesn't see or store your card details — those are handled entirely by our payment partner. We store only what's needed to identify a transaction and, for professionals, the bank account payouts are sent to.

While a pilot or manual process is in place

Where Sorted discloses that a particular payment flow is manual or pilot-only (for example, for a new category or a new market), that will be stated clearly before you pay, along with what that means for how and when funds move.

5.Delivery and pickup

For categories where Sorted arranges pickup and delivery (for example, laundry and dry cleaning), we share the pickup and drop-off address and a contact phone number with our delivery partner so they can dispatch a courier. Delivery timing depends on the courier and isn't guaranteed to the minute.

6.Fees

Sorted charges a platform fee on completed gigs, shown before you pay — currently up to 10% of the gig's bounty, and waived for categories where Sorted already arranges paid delivery. Fees can change; the fee that applies to a gig is the one shown at the time you approve payment for it.

7.Cancellations, disputes and refunds

If something's wrong with submitted work, raise a dispute before approving it — approving payment releases funds and can't be undone through the app. A dispute freezes the gig so no payment moves while it's reviewed.

If a dispute is resolved in the client's favor, no charge has occurred, so there's nothing to refund — the gig closes unpaid. If it's resolved in the professional's favor, Sorted will prompt the client to pay, but Nigerian payment rails mean we generally can't force a charge on a client who doesn't cooperate; in that case, enforcement is through the professional's and client's record on Sorted, not a guaranteed financial outcome.

Outside a dispute, gigs can be cancelled before work begins by agreement between both sides.
